It's been **a year** since I joined this unit **in March**. **I had** a lot of experiences. Sometimes time slipped away fast, but sometimes, it didn't.

I'm finally about to be promoted **to the final rank** in **the Korean army: sergeant**. I've heard **that "sergeant" is the wrong translation in Korean**; however, I couldn't care less about it.

When **it had** been a couple of months since I enlisted, I didn't picture the future **in which I'd become a sergeant**. In Korea, sergeant is not a unique rank, because **all men in Korea are required to serve in the army and are discharged as sergeants unless they have an exceptional problem**. Turns out there are **tons of sergeants in society**, and it's not a big deal.

**Discharged men no longer receive any special social recognition**. The reason **I've looked forward to the promotion** is simply that I wanted to give myself some credit for enduring **a long time** in the army.

I used to think that **it gave me a sense of comfort, as if I were accomplishing a kind of common triumph that everyone achieves**. I just hope that I **can get through ordinary things that everyone has to go through**. Hence, being a sergeant is meaningful to me for that reason.

Anyway, **becoming a sergeant** means that there are about 160 days left until I'll be discharged. My **worries about who I'll become** after that **are justified**. But I can't complain. No wonder I was looking forward to my discharge. That calls for a **celebration**. **All of my concerns** will be history.

Such a long period of **seemingly endless remaining days has gradually passed**. I don't want to **look miserable** or pity my previous days. They were worth it. The **existence** of this posting **proves** it.

Oh well, at least I haven't lost any **physical things before** I get promoted to the final rank in **the navy**. Let's just hope that nothing bad **happens** to me.
