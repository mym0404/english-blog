I took a winter snowboarding trip with military roommates. It was an overnight trip from January 26 to 27. I hadn't snowboarded in a year. At the top of the first slope, I couldn't remember how to ride the board. As a result, it didn't matter. How to ride the board was carved into my brain and I managed to ride down the mountain pretty well.

As I rode the slopes, I decided that I had to improve my snowboarding skills. I wanted to learn a more advanced skill called carving. Carving is a sort of skill that allows you to ride the board using its side edges. In contrast to a skid turn, carving uses only the edges. It doesn't lose the speed of the snowboard by minimizing friction. For carving, I needed more strength to press edges to the snow. It caused me to get some muscle aches. 

Frankly, I fell over a plenty of times. I was required to go to the hospital and to capture my born using X-ray. If you are intersted in snowboarding, it might be a good idea to prepare some high quality protect equipments such as butt and knee shield.

In spite of injuries, I'd say I wanna ride again. Snowboarding engages my competitiveness and perseverance. Those are my favorites and I can conquer how to ride and, mountain, the nature. 

Given this trip, 10 members joined. The half of them rode ski or snowboard on the second day not only the first day. On the night of the first day, we got together and drunk some alcohols. One of members prepared enjoyable recreation for us, and it gave a lot of fun to us.

I have a plan after discharging the military. I'll buy deck for snowboard and get a lift pass for the next winter season, and go to the mountain every week. It sounds exhausting. But I want to improve my snowboarding skills. Moreover, I'm enjoying the feeling that I conquer the mountain itself.
