Not gonna lie, time literally flies in June. It's been imperative that I 've had to make quite a few decisions or events. Let's lay it out.

# Ordering a Brand-New MacBook

Can you believe that this is my fourth MacBook in my life? I started using a MacBook in about 2017, and it's already been 10 years!

It's not like I don't like Windows, but the keyboard layout on Windows doesn't feel the same when I type on macOS anymore.

![[20260620_102051.png]]

I failed to figure out why shipping would take more than half a month. Is it affect by a RAM shortage? I don't know. On the bright side, I wouldn't be able to use it for now because I'm in the army. 

**I paid for it in one payment.** There is a backstory. I wanted to pay for it in 3 installments.  I lowered my credit card spending limit because my machine got hacked and felt nervous about it.

But I never realized that I couldn't raise it again at will. Back in the day, I raised my debit card spending limit, so I thought it applied to the credit card similarly. What a stupid. 

It was definitely a fallacy in my brain and I learned a lesson through real-life experience. Thank you.

Okay, enough of payment. One of my mottos is the following.

> The faster the CPU you have, the better your performance at work becomes.
 
Today, some ridiculous phenomena have emerged as AI-agents have evolved. Developers turn on YouTube while their AI agents work from time to time. What the heck.

A faster CPU gets rid of the gap between your reasoning trails during the development process. If building projects and reports from AI-agents become faster, then you can stay focused on your work. Right?

I believe that this positive cycle affects not only local focus intensity but also overall performance eventually.

# Interview and Chats 

Recently, I can't stop thinking about my next career move.

As a developer who wanted to become a superman like [John Carmack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carmack), I sometimes think "Is it the right fit for me?" Am I passionate enough to construct "The Great Thing"? What drives me to shove myself into the deep immersion?

Finally, I decided that it's mandatory to find a great leader who can pivot and suggest the next destination, S-class teammates who can give me precise feedback, and a truly immersive environment. I believe those help me feel motivated 24/7 on my work.

> I mentioned those desires exactly in [my previous book report](https://mjstudio.net/book/review/7-no-rule) 18 months ago! Didn't I?

If you ask me, I'm not that selfish. As of me, entering a company and an office means give-and-take stuff. If they give me something I want, I'm obliged to return something more valuable inspiring to them. Reasonable?

In that context, I've been trying to have a soft spot for sharing great conversations with a few developers, leaders, interviewers. Actually, it's so much fun. I can't get enough of it. I can do it all day long, discussing frameworks and sharing ideas.

From there, I got into some new topics and got involved in them. I've learned a bunch of things and achieved some triumphs there. Yup, I've grown. Can my heart start racing again?
